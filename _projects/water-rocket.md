For Maker Faire 2025, I worked with Suleiman Mohiuddin and Jay Kim to make a physical multistage water rocket and launch system. After we built the prototype, I re designed the rocket in CAD to make its staged form and connections clearer.

<dl class="meta">
  <div><dt>Methods</dt><dd>Physical prototyping, CAD, electronics</dd></div>
  <div><dt>Tools</dt><dd>Tinkercad, Arduino</dd></div>
</dl>

## 1. Process

<div class="project-step physical-step" markdown="1">
<div class="step-copy" markdown="1">

We began by making the rocket physically. The built prototype brought together the body, the launch stand, and an Arduino based controller with a display and servo. Starting with a real assembly gave me a concrete reference for the next iteration rather than treating the rocket as a purely digital idea.

<div class="project-step cad-step" markdown="1">
<div class="step-copy" markdown="1">

After the physical prototype, I re designed the rocket in CAD. I modeled the two stages as connected pieces, then worked through the body sections, fins, coupler, and controller enclosure as distinct elements. The CAD model made the relationship between the stages visible and gives the design a clear basis for another physical version.

## 2. Final Design

<div class="project-step final-step" markdown="1">
<div class="step-copy" markdown="1">

The project now holds both stages of the work: a physical prototype that was built and tested first, and a later CAD redesign that clarifies the multistage assembly. Making the prototype before modeling it gave the re design a direct connection to the real rocket, launch stand, and controller rather than starting from an abstract concept.
