A self watering plant pot I designed and 3D printed for my Maker Faire biology project. The body is a simple cylinder that holds the plant and a water reservoir below it, and a small round display set into the front reports how the plant is doing. The idea was that the pot should answer the two questions you actually have about a plant, whether it needs water and whether it is too hot or too cold, without you having to dig into the soil to find out.

## 01 Form

I kept the outside as quiet as possible. It is a straight cylinder with a softly rounded base and a recessed lip at the top, so the pot reads as an object you would leave on a shelf rather than a piece of equipment. Everything technical sits inside or behind the one round screen, which is the only thing that breaks the surface. Keeping the wall simple also made it straightforward to print.

## 02 Interface

The home screen splits into the two things worth knowing at a glance. Water sits on the left as a ring gauge with a percentage and a plain word reading, and temperature sits on the right with a value and whether that value is in a good range. Underneath, a single line sums it up in language anyone can read rather than making you interpret the numbers. I used a leaf and a droplet as the only icons so the screen stays legible at that size.

![Home screen reading water at 70 percent and temperature at 24 degrees, with the plant status underneath](/assets/projects/3d-printed-plant-pot/design-03.png)
![The same screen from a lower angle, showing how it sits flush in the surface](/assets/projects/3d-printed-plant-pot/design-04.png)

## 03 Reservoir Screen

Tapping through to water opens the detail view at the top of this page. It gives the same percentage as a larger ring, then adds the number you need when you are standing there with a watering can, how many litres are actually left. A back control returns to the home screen, so the whole interface is only two levels deep.
